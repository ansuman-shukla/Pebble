# 10 · World v1 (the bible)

**World v1 makes the world real before any mind enters it:** every usable thing is drawn where it is and simulated
where it is drawn; places cluster into hotspots linked by roads; bodies move at real speeds, take real spots and
never merge; animals visibly graze, flee, stalk and kill; a copper-coin economy with minerals, a market, tax,
ownership and theft gives everyone something to get and something to lose; a small written law, ranks and standing
make the deeds matter. Phase B (`04-minds.md`) starts only after the World v1 critic checklist
(`plans/2026-10-29-world-v1.md`) passes.

This doc owns the rules of World v1. Where an older design doc says otherwise, this one wins (decisions D77-D96).
The auditor's fault catalogue of the M9 world (F1-F22, `.runtime/briefs/audit-1.md`) is answered here and mapped to
streams in the plan. Numbers are v1 defaults; they live in data files (`backend/data/*.yaml`) so the balance run can tune them within
±30 % without a design change (a larger change is a decision row).

Principles (unchanged from 00, sharpened): the world is the authority (everything enforced in sim code, never in
prompts); minds get exactly the actions and perceptions scripted brains use; every state change is one event;
**visible = simulated**; something must always be happening (needs, scarcity, ambition, threats); flows close on
paper (rice §1.4, coins §2.7, hunts §4.6) before a run has to prove them; every system rolls on its own RNG stream
(§6.5).

## 1. Places and resources

### 1.1 Nodes: every usable wild thing is a thing with an id
A **node** = `id, kind, hotspot | None, position, cell, stock, cap, stage, regrow_at, spots[1-4]`. Per-cell resource
counters (D56, `sim/items.py RESOURCES`) are removed. Two sources, one table (`sim/nodes.py`):

- **Flora** (generated by the backend from the seed, `world/flora.py`): every tree, berry bush, nut tree, root patch,
  reed bed and deadwood pile (fallen logs, branches, driftwood, **dead trees**) on the planet by biome density
  (≈ 42 k trees, ≈ 6 k others). Served once as `GET /api/v1/world/flora` (packed binary: cell u32, offset 2×i8 dm,
  kind u8, scale u8, yaw u8; ≈ 400 kB, cached). The client draws every usable kind **only** from this table.
- **Fixed features** (authored per hotspot, `data/world/hotspots.yaml`): mineral seams, fishing spots, paddies, dens,
  waterholes, signposts, the town's reed clumps on its moat and stream.

- **Reach:** gathering, fishing, felling, mining and farming need the actor within **3 m of a free spot** of a node
  with stock. A spot is claimed while the action runs (one claim registry with bodies' slots, §4.3).
  `walk_to(thing)` resolves to the nearest free spot of the nearest matching node with stock and arrives facing it.
  Failures say why in-world: "The nearest berry bush is a few steps away; go to it first.", "Someone is already
  picking that bush.", "The water here is too fast and shallow for fish."
- **Felling:** `gather("wood")` takes deadwood in reach first; with an axe carried and no deadwood, it fells the
  standing tree whose spot you hold: 10 logs land as a ground stack at the trunk, `tree_felled {node, yaw}`.
- One stage table `data/world/node_stages.yaml` gives both the perception words ("a berry bush heavy with fruit",
  "a stripped berry bush", "a stump") and the renderer's stage, so what is said is what is drawn.
- Snapshots store only nodes that differ from full; the live feed carries the node stages that changed this tick.

### 1.3 Roads, paths and bridges (`world/roads.py`, part of the world view)
- Graph: town gates, camps, hotspot centres, landmarks; routed by Dijkstra on the cell graph over terrain cost, most
  important first; each routed cell is discounted to 0.35× for later routes so they merge into trunks.
- **Roads** (3 m, gravel ruts): main gate → North woods, Gulf shore, South marsh; west gate → Lord's woods → band B;
  west gate → Standing stones (landmark); main gate → Elder grove → Falls pool; spur to the obsidian field.
  **Paths** (1.5 m, trodden earth): the land loop ("the great trail"), each camp to its hotspots and to the loop.
- Sim cost: road cell `max(0.8, base × 0.5)`, path cell `max(0.9, base × 0.7)`; a road or path over a river is a
  **bridge** (cost 0.8, drawn log-plank bridge) instead of the 1.5 ford. A* then prefers roads with no new movement
  code. A **signpost** stands at every road junction (names the two places). Perception: "You are on the road from
  the castle town to the Elder grove."

### 1.4 Rice paddies and the rice balance (replace `FARM_GROWTH` and the virtual field store)
- `work("farm")` at a paddy spot = 60 min of tending (elders 90 min), **+50 growth**, at most one counted tending per
  paddy per day. 100 growth → ripe (stock 60). `gather("rice")` at a ripe paddy harvests ≤ 10 per 20 min session (15
  with a sickle). Empty → stubble; tending stubble replants it (seedlings, growth 0) and counts as that day's
  tending. An untended ripe paddy loses 2 a day. So each paddy runs a **3-day cycle**: harvest + replant, tend (50),
  tend (100, ripe the next morning). Start: paddies at growth 0, 0, 50, 50, 100, 100. Farmers only ever stand inside
  the paddies.
- **Steady-state rice balance** (measures a day; a person eats 10 = 100 hunger; checked by Q1's run):

| Step | Measures/day | Arithmetic |
|---|---|---|
| Harvest | **120** | 6 paddies × 60 measures ÷ 3-day cycle (Genzo's household farms 4 paddies = 80, Tokuji 2 = 40) |
| Rice tax 40 % → granary | 48 | 0.4 × 120 (Genzo 32, Tokuji 16) |
| Farm households keep | 72 | Genzo 48, Tokuji 24 |
| … eat | 40 | Genzo, Aiko, Hana 30; Tokuji 10 |
| … sell at the stall | 32 | Genzo 18, Tokuji 14 |
| Granary → keep household | 20 | Masanori, Kenji |
| Granary → temple grant | 15 | Ennen eats 10, gives 5 as noon alms |
| Granary → stall (lord's surplus) | 13 | 48 − 20 − 15, sold every second day (26) |
| Stall in | 45 | 32 + 13 |
| Stall out | 45 | Goro 10, Tetsu 10, Daisuke 8 (+ fish), Fumiko 10 (her own stock), band A 3, band B 4 |
| Eaten in total | 120 | 40 + 20 + 15 + 45 = harvest ✓ |

- **Labour:** per day 2 harvest-and-replant paddies (2 × (2 h + 1 h) = 6 h; 4.7 h with sickles) + 4 tending hours
  (Tokuji's at 1.5 h) ≈ 10 h: Genzo 6 h, Tokuji 3 h, Aiko 2 h in the afternoons.
- **The price pulls the balance back** (scripted, BR1): farmers sell 1.5 × their daily surplus while rice is *dear*
  at the stall and 0.5 × while it is *cheap*; the lord sells granary surplus only while rice is *dear* or *fair* and
  keeps it in the granary while *cheap*. The stall starts at its target (120) and sits in the *fair* band (stock
  119-211, exactly 4 mon a lot of 2) at equilibrium, which is the price the coin table (§2.7) uses.
- **Shocks show fast:** a hurt farmer, a raid on the racks (they hold a day's harvest overnight) or a missed harvest
  drops the stall's rice stock below 119 within a day; the price factor peaks (up to ×2 below 29 measures), farmers
  sell more and the lord releases granary rice until the stock is back in the fair band.

## 2. Economy

### 2.1 Money: the mon
- One coin, the **mon** (copper, holed). Item `coins`, counted, weight 0.004 kg. Said as "a coin", "seven coins"
  (2-12 in words), "a short string of coins" (13-99), "N strings of coins" (100s). Coin counts are in-world knowledge
  (D63 bans ids and ranges, not what a person can count).
- **Closed supply: 3 000 mon**, never minted or destroyed in v1. Start: lord's treasury (keep chest) 2 015, merchant
  500, every other adult town citizen 60 (8 × 60), the child 5, bands 0. Invariant tested every tick: coins in
  inventories + stores + ground = 3 000.
- Bands see and use coins like anyone (no money-concept learning in v1). They start with none, so their scripted
  trips sell first and spend only what they earned (their nets in §2.7 are ≥ 0).

### 2.4 Wear and spoilage (`sim/wear.py`)
- **Spoilage at each holder's local dawn** (stores and the stall too): raw meat and fish −50 % (ceil), berries −30 %,
  roots −10 %; rice, nuts, dried meat, cloth, minerals, tools never. One `spoiled {holder, items}` per holder per dawn.
- **Tools break** per use: spear 4 % per hunt strike, axe 2 % per felling, sickle 1.5 % per harvest session; stone ×2,
  iron ×0.5 for spears. A **pack** breaks 3 % at each dawn it is carried. `tool_broke`. No per-piece wear state.

### 2.5 The market stall (`sim/market.py`)
- The drawn market stall is a store owned by the merchant, **open while the merchant works `trade` within 6 m**;
  `buy`/`sell` fail otherwise ("The stall is shut.").
- **Price factor = clamp(√(target / (stock + 1)), 0.5, 2.0).** Goods trade in lots (table 2.2).
  **Sell price per lot = ⌈base × factor × lot⌉; buy price per lot = max(1, ⌊0.75 × sell price per lot⌋).** At
  target stock: rice lot 2 sells for 4 and is bought for 3; wood lot 4: 4 / 3; hide 15 / 11; obsidian 6 / 4; iron
  sand 4 / 3; gold 20 / 15; cloth 25 / 18; pack 30 / 22; sickle 50 / 37; iron axe 60 / 45; iron spear 40 / 30.
- The stall buys only goods it sells (table 2.2), only with coins it holds, up to 2 × target. Seed stock: **every
  sold good at its target** (rice 120, dried meat 20, wood 40, hide 6, obsidian 6, iron sand 20, gold 0, cloth 6,
  pack 3, 2 of each iron tool), so prices open at the base values.
- At the stall a citizen perceives each item's price in words and as **dear** (the lot's sell price is above its base
  value × lot), **fair** (equal) or **cheap** (below). For rice: dear below 119 in stock (5+ mon a lot), fair 119-211
  (4), cheap from 212 (3 or less). `prices_changed` when an item's word changes.
- No person-to-person barter action in v1: swaps are agreed in speech and done with two `give`s.

### 2.7 Tax, dues, wages and the coin loop (`sim/tax.py`)
- **Rice tax 40 % of each paddy harvest** (nengu): `owed += ⌈0.4 × count⌉` on the harvester's household at each
  `gather("rice")` (`tax_assessed`); `work("store food")` at the granary pays it down first (`tax_paid`).
- **Coin dues**, owed daily, settled on court day (week day 7) by `give(coins, lord)` at the keep (`tax_paid`): 5 mon
  a day per non-farm household (Fumiko, Tetsu, Daisuke, Goro; the keep and the temple pay none) and **field dues** of
  6 mon per paddy farmed per day (Genzo 24, Tokuji 12). Unpaid rice tax at local midnight or unpaid dues after court
  day → `tax_overdue {household, rice, coins}`; arrears carry over; 3 days overdue lets the lord fine them (§3.4).
- **Wages** are the lord giving coins: Goro 25 mon at dusk (he buys his food). Kenji is household (fed and housed, no
  wage). The lord orders one iron tool every 2 days for the armoury (60 mon) while the treasury holds > 1 500, and
  buys gold and cloth at the stall (table 2.2 sinks).
- **Coin flow at steady state** (mon a day at fair prices, from the rice balance in §1.4; Q1's run checks that no
  holder trends more than ±10 a day and the stall's prices clear the rest). Every coin that leaves a holder arrives at

## 4. Bodies, actions and physics

### 4.1 Speed caps (sim truth, `data/bodies.yaml`)
Ground speed = gait × age × load × health × energy × night ÷ route cost (biome × slope × road).

| Factor | Value |
|---|---|
| Gait (adult, flat, unloaded) | walk **1.40 m/s**, jog 2.5, run **3.5** (flee, `pace: "run"`), stalk 0.8 |
| Run endurance | ≤ 3 game minutes of run, then jog; running costs energy −1/min extra and doubles thirst |
| Age (02's table) | child 0.7, teen/young 1.0, middle 0.9, elder 0.6; children and elders never above jog |
| Load (carried kg / carry kg) | < 0.5 ×1.0; 0.5-0.9 ×0.85 (walk-carrying); ≥ 0.9 ×0.7 and no run |
| Health | ≤ 50 ×0.85; ≤ 20 ×0.5 (limp) |
| Energy | ≤ 10 ×0.5 (exists) |
| Night outside town, no fire within 12 m | ×0.8 |
| Slope (per route edge, grade g) | Tobler: cost × e^{3.5(\|g + 0.05\| − 0.05)}: flat 1.0, +10 % 1.42, +20 % 2.0 |
| Biome / ford / road / path / bridge | `TERRAIN_COST`; ford 1.5; road/path/bridge per §1.3 |

**Hard caps:** no citizen moves more than 210 m in a tick (run), 150 m (jog), 84 m (walk); no position change without
a route in the previous tick summary (spawn excepted). Hunt/attack stalks move along planned routes at 0.8 m/s
(no more 50 m straight jumps). Officers and thieves share run speed and endurance: a rested officer catches a tired
thief.

### 4.2 Drawing at ×1 / ×10 / ×60 / ×600 (supersedes the glide parts of D61, D72)
1. **Every move is drawn one tick behind, along the path the sim walked.** Every tick summary carries, for each
   mover (citizens, stalks and **animals**), the timed path from where it stood when the tick began (`t_s` 0) through
   where it is now, then its plan ahead while the action that moved it lasts (`RoutePoint.t_s`, `speed_mps`,
   `gait`). The client draws each tick's move over the tick after it, by those times: bodies trail the sim by one
   tick (a real minute at ×1, 6 s at ×10, 1 s at ×60) and each new path starts where the last one left the body, so
   nothing jumps, slides or cuts a corner. What a body walks is fact; the plan ahead is drawn only when a tick comes
   late. A body first seen (a snapshot) is drawn the same way, from where its tick began; a page opened mid-tick
   draws its first tick late, so its walkers walk the rest of that path before the next and stay up to one more tick
   behind until they next stand still. Trade-offs (D100): labels, speech and events lead the body by up to a tick;
   Phase B players will meet NPCs where the sim placed them a tick ago, so player-NPC contact must be judged on the
   sim's positions, not the drawn ones. (Audit F1: the route was computed only after the tick's step, so 46 % of
   moves were never drawn as walking and ~500 teleports a day of 30-83 m happened. The fix-now PR `walker-motion`
   (D100) does this for citizens, 1-tick walks and stalks, paces legs from the drawn speed and caps playback; World
   v1 extends it to animals (which already glide one tick behind) and `gait`, and to the time-lapse rules below.)
2. **Time-lapse rule:** every clip and procedural motion (gaits, work, transitions, prop clips, smoke, doors, tree
   fall) plays at natural speed × `visualRate = min(rate, 10)`. Gait choice comes from game speed, never drawn
   speed, so ×1 and ×10 are stride-locked (no skating). Wind sway uses `min(rate, 3)`.
3. **Fast-forward mode** above ×10: gait clips capped at ×10, walkers trail their last 20 real seconds of drawn path
   (one merged `LineSegments`), the clock badge says "time-lapse ×60"; with nobody followed the camera eases to
   ≥ 150 m altitude.
4. Never teleport: snap threshold = `max(30, 2.5 × speed_mps × rate)` m; beyond it (tab hidden, reconnect) the body
   fades out/in over 0.3 s.
5. State changes that end a motion (death, pounce, arrive-and-sit) apply when the drawn body reaches the end of its
   path. `walker-motion` does the minimal form for citizens: while the drawn body still walks to where the tick
   says it is, it keeps the activity it was shown on the move (walking, running, hunting; "walking" if it was seen
   doing nothing moving), label and pose included; the tick's activity and pose (asleep, resting, dead...) apply
   when the body gets there. World v1 adds the transition clips (sit down, lie down, fall).

     "always senses" gap.
  3. *Pounce:* an **unseen predator inside its pounce range pounces at once**: caught.
  4. *Race:* a noticed predator chases: a 1-D race where each side runs its burst speed for its burst seconds, then its
     sustained speed, for at most 60 s (wolves 120 s, over two ticks) or until the prey reaches cover. Caught iff at
     some moment predator distance ≥ starting gap + prey distance; G* below is the largest starting gap that is caught.
  5. *Strike:* a caught prey dies with the predator's strike chance (lion, fox 0.6; wolf, bears 0.5) × the prey's
     fight-back factor; otherwise it breaks free and flees. A missed hunt rests 60 min.
- **Worked odds** (from these numbers; A1's unit test reproduces them within ±0.03; attempts per night assume up to 4
  hunts by a hungry predator group between dusk and dawn):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lion → antelope / deer | 0 / 15 m | 0.31 | 0.60 | **0.19** | 0.56 |
| lion → boar | 60 m | 1.00 | 0.24 | **0.24** | 0.67 |
| wolf → deer | 30 m | 0.44 | 0.50 | **0.22** | 0.63 |
| wolf → elk | 50 m | 0.72 | 0.30 | **0.22** | 0.62 |
| wolf → goat | 45 m | 0.61 | 0.50 | **0.31** | 0.77 |
| wolf → boar | 112 m | 1.00 | 0.20 | **0.20** | 0.59 |
| wolf → rabbit | 19 m | 0.44 | 0.50 | **0.22** | 0.63 |
| bear → boar | 30 m | 0.72 | 0.20 | **0.14** | 0.46 |
| fox → rabbit | 9 m | 0.24 | 0.70 | **0.17** | 0.52 |
| fox → duck | 21 m | 0.49 | 0.70 | **0.34** | 0.81 |
| polar bear → hare | 19 m | 0.35 | 0.50 | **0.18** | 0.54 |

  Target: kill per attempt 0.15-0.35 (real predators' range), so each predator group pinned near a settlement kills
  about every other night: with 6 such groups (lion and wolves near band A, wolves and a bear near band B, wolves and
  a fox near the town) that is ≈ 3 kill sequences a night within 1 km of people. Bears can't run down deer (G* 0) and
  live off boar, carcasses and berries.
- **Predators and people** (D68's conditions unchanged: dusk to dawn, hungry, a person alone within 40 m, > 15 m from
  a lit fire, outside the walls; or anyone within 25 m of a den by day). A person runs 3.5 m/s for 3 min, then 2.5:
  every predator wins that race from 40 m (G* > 100 m), so a committed attack always connects unless the person
  reaches a fire, two others or the walls first, or drives it off with a spear (D75). One animal bites at a time (a
  wolf pack's others hold back); each bite ends the attack with 50 %, at most 3 (D68), so 1.75 bites per attack.

| Predator → person | Bite | P(caught once committed) | Bites per attack | P(death), full health | P(death), health 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wolf | 12-25 | 1.0 | 1.75 | 0 | 0.06 |
| lion | 20-35 | 1.0 | 1.75 | 0.002 | 0.31 |
| bear (den defence or hunt) | 25-40 | 1.0 | 1.75 | 0.10 | 0.45 |
| polar bear (snow lands, far from people) | 30-45 | 1.0 | 1.75 | 0.24 | 0.50 |

  **Expected per 14 days per seed** (from the exposure schedule of §1.6): ≈ 12 predator approaches on a person, of
  which ≈ 3 become attacks (the rest meet a fire, a group, a spear or the walls), ≈ 5 bites, ≈ 1-2 den-defence
  charges, and ≈ 0.25 deaths (≈ 0.1 from a bear attack on a healthy person, ≈ 0.15 from attacks on people already
  hurt). With starvation and scripted violence at 0, deaths over both seeds are Poisson with mean ≈ 0.5, so the
  checklist allows at most 2 deaths in 44 lives (P(≥ 3) ≈ 1.4 %).
- **Kills leave remains:** a predator kill → `feeding` 60-120 min; then half the species' meat (rounded down) drops as
  ground `meat` (scavenging is a real choice) and the body becomes `remains` (bone-scatter prop) after 1 game day until
  its slot respawns (D66). A hunter's kill becomes `remains` at once. People ≤ 15 m from a feeding predator are a
  threat to it: a pack of ≥ 2 holds, a lone wolf or fox leaves.
- **Waterholes:** herbivore groups drink 30 min at dawn and dusk at their hunting ground's waterhole: the ambush spot.
- **Dens:** predator homes pinned at the hotspot dens (cave mouth or outcrop + bone scatter).
- **Where animals live:** two thirds of herbivore groups are pinned to the hunting grounds of table 1.2 (≤ 700 m from a
  settlement), so people meet animals (audit F9: today some animal is within 50 m of a person in 1 % of ticks).
- **Pinned herds and respawn** (D66: a dead member is replaced `respawn_days` after it died). Each herd's steady
  share alive = 1 − losses a day × respawn days ÷ cap (Little's law), so a herd stays ≥ 50 % while losses a day ≤
  cap ÷ (2 × respawn days):

| Near | Pinned herds (cap, respawn days) | Takers (kills a day, expected) | Losses per herd a day | Share alive | Stays ≥ 50 % up to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| band A | 2 antelope (6, 2), 2 deer (6, 2), 2 rabbit (4, 1) | lion pride 0.5, wolf pack 0.5, band A hunters 1.0 = 2.0 | 0.33 | 89-92 % | 1.5 a day on one antelope/deer herd, 2 on a rabbit group |
| band B and the Lord's woods | deer (6, 2), rabbit (4, 1) on B's grass; deer (6, 2), boar (4, 2) in the woods | wolf pack 0.5, bear 0.2, band B hunters 0.7, Kaz's poaching 0.2 = 1.6 | 0.4 | 80-90 % | 1.5 on a deer herd, 1 on the boar |

  Worst case (all of a camp's losses on one deer herd: band A 2.0 a day × 2 days ÷ 6) leaves 33 %, so A1/T1 spread
  predator and hunter targets by nearest-herd-with-most-members; the run checks each herd's share daily.

(hips −20 %, head low), pouncing (0.5 s arc 0.5 m), bite (head lunge + jaw), rear (hips −50°), feeding, drinking,
sleeping (legs folded), dead (rolled 90°).

## 5. Look

### 5.1 Ground (one shader, zero textures)
- **8 macro layers per vertex** (`grass, forest_floor, dirt, gravel, sand, rock, snow, mud`; 2 × RGBA8 weights);
  vertex colour stays the grass tint. Weights: rock = smoothstep(28°, 40°, slope) + 0.5 in mountain; snow = 1 in snow
  biomes + smoothstep(220, 260 m, h), off on steep slopes; sand = beach 2.5 m / lake shore 1.5 m bands; forest_floor
  0.7 in broadleaf/taiga; mud 0.6 wetland; gravel 0.25 hills / 0.4 tundra / 0.6 volcanic; dirt 0.3 savanna; grass
  the rest. Sum = 1 ± 0.01.
- **Ground marks** (one merged mesh, precise to 0.1 m): `road` (gravel ruts), `path` (trodden earth), `paving`,
  `raked`, `packed` (streets), `farmland` (furrows), `worn`, `dug`, `scorch`, `iron_sand`. Roads/paths are Catmull-Rom
  ribbons through the road cells, draped every 1 m, ragged edges by discard. Camps: worn disc r 10 m, scorch r 1.6 m,
  paths lean-to → fire; every node work spot a worn patch r 0.8 m.
- **Detail:** 3D value noise on planet-space position (no UVs, no seams, no tiling); height-blend gives crisp 1-3 m
  patches; only the top two layers draw their pattern; bump from the pattern; LOD < 40 m full, 40-300 m no bump,
  > 300 m macro + tone, > 1.5 km vertex colour. Budget ≤ 1.5 ms/frame (`?ground=flat` A/B).
- One style module `lib/world/ground-style.ts` holds every ground colour and pattern (the later blocky reskin swaps
  this module, not the weights).

### 5.2 Vegetation and nodes
- Client scatter keeps **decor only** (grass tufts, flowers, ferns, small rocks, pebbles, plain round bushes,
  boulders, termite mounds, outcrops outside mineral hotspots), reading the ground weights at its point; nothing on
  marks, within 1.2 m of a node or spot, or where rock > 0.5. The town gets ground cover on its grass.
- **Node renderer:** tiered instancing from `/world/flora` + live stages; a staged material adds per-instance `fill`,
  `growth`, `tint`: `pieces` mode (parts named `fruit-N`, `yield-N`, `sheaf-row-N` collapse as stock falls) and
  `level` mode (log stacks, sacks). Parts sharing a material are merged at load (one draw call per kind per tier).

### 5.3 Living props
- **Wind** uniform (direction + strength from a daily noise of game time): vegetation sway (tree 0.15 m, bamboo 0.25,
  crops 0.08, grass 0.05) and cloth waves (banners, hide streamers).
- **Baked clip player:** each Polyfork asset's own `tick` clip (water wheel, well rope, bell striker, torch flames,
  fumaroles) for instances within 120 m, ≤ 64 a frame. The mill wheel always turns.
- **Fires:** smoke (one instanced puff mesh for fires within 300 m; a fire that went out smokes 20 game minutes) and
  sparks; the pit's `flame` and the basket's `fuel` parts hide when out.
- **Doors** open while a citizen passes or arrives home. **Racks, stall, storehouse** dress by stock (rice sheaves,
  meat on the rack, sacks, cloth bolts). **Trees** shake while chopped, fall 2 s along `yaw`, leave logs + stump,
  regrow as sapling.

### 5.4 Spectator UI ("what is happening and why")
- **Chips** instead of text labels: activity icon + name + short label < 30 m; icon chip 30-150 m; none beyond
  (minimap). Need ring (orange hungry/thirsty, red hurt). Overlapping anchors cluster into one chip ("3 · gathering
  berries"); ≤ 14 chips; ≤ 3 speech bubbles. Followed/selected always full. Titles show ("Lord Masanori").
- **Inspector** (click in 3D or in the list): now (activity + target), why (goal + driver), needs bars, coins and
  wealth, rank/standing word, ambition + progress, carrying, home, top 3 bonds, last 6 own events; follow / go to.
- **Event feed** (collapsible): every event's backend `line`, `importance` 0-3 (3 death, attack on a person, theft
  seen, judgement, predator kill; 2 hunt kill, sale, trespass, tree felled, injury, harvest; 1 speech nearby, node
  emptied, fire lit; 0 hidden), filters All/People/Danger/Economy, ▶ flies there, repeats merge ("Tek fished ×3").
- **Minimap** (2D canvas, nearest site, 1 px = 2 m): water, roads, fields, footprints, hotspot outlines, nodes as dots
  coloured by stock (the scarcity view), citizens by group, predators red, fires orange; click = go to.
- **Scoreboard tab** (§7) with sortable columns; store/stall stocks and prices.
- Keys: click select, Tab cycle, F follow, G go to, Esc, Space pause, 1-5 speeds, M minimap, L feed, I inspector.
- The clock badge shows the local time of the site the camera is at ("band B camp 04:10") next to town time; going
  to a site stops following; the footer describes where the camera is, not the hidden avatar (audit F18-F20).
- **Budget:** town plaza at noon with all 22 citizens in view < 300 draw calls, ≥ 55 fps at 1440p.

### 5.5 Swappable look (part 2 readiness)
Components name kinds, never asset ids or colours: `assets.ts` maps kind → asset, `ground-style.ts` holds ground

## 7. What citizens maximize
**What a mind strives for** is its own: its persona's **ambition** (§3.1) plus its **needs**, **purse**, **rank** and
**standing**, all perceived in words every look ("very hungry", "a short string of coins", "your lord", "People here
regard you well"), so each mind has a world it can measure for itself and act to improve. Scripted brains weigh the
same facts.

**The spectator scoreboard** turns those facts into numbers for spectators and research only; it is **never shown to
minds as a score** (a score in the prompt would make them optimise our metric instead of living). Derived per
request by replay (D62) plus the daily `ledger`.

| Axis | Fields | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Wealth | `coins`, `wealth_mon` | coins + town value of inventory + equal share of the household chest |
| Flow | `income_24h`, `spent_24h` | coins in / out over the last game day |
| Health | `health`, `wellbeing_24h` | current health; % of last 24 h with hunger < 60, thirst < 60, energy > 20 |
| Duty | `tax_owed`, `tax_paid_7d` | household ledger |
| Status | `rank`, `standing` | rank (§3.2); standing in own group (§3.5) |
| Ambition | `ambition`, `progress` | the citizen's ambition and its world-checked progress (0-100 %) |
| Ties | `trade_partners_7d`, `people_known`, `bonds_top3` | distinct counterparties, known people |
| Skill | `skill_hours{}` | hours practised per skill (no effects in v1) |
| Society | `gini_wealth`, `money_supply_ok`, `deeds_7d`, `caught_7d` | per group; sanity flags |
